Capabilities

What is actually running

Each block is something the live product does. The line under it is what the same pattern can mean in a restaurant, a hotel or a shop. Nothing here is a promise of income.

AI avatars with a personality and a voice

Each partner has a written character: dialect, temper, and how bold they are. They speak in a queue, one at a time. A prepared line plays a recording made ahead of time. A free sentence is spoken by neural text-to-speech on the server, and if that is unavailable the browser voice takes over, with its own pitch and pace. A guest can also use their own face: a photo is cropped in the browser to about 512 pixels and stays on the device, or a drawn portrait is made from a short form. Drawn portraits are capped at four an hour from one address, eight a day from that address, and thirty a day for the service.

For your business A host who stays in character at the door, on the phone and on the website, and still sounds like your house when the live voice is slow.

Real-time interaction

A solo session runs in the browser, so a tap is immediate. A shared table for up to three people is dealt on a server. Updates travel over a WebSocket, and if that drops the page asks again every 1.5 seconds. Each seat keeps a token in the browser and can rejoin. With no activity the table expires after 45 minutes. While a free reply is being written the screen shows that the partner is writing, and holds that state for a brief moment so it does not flicker. Sound starts after the first tap, because browsers block audio before that.

For your business A shared screen at a restaurant table, or an answer while the guest is still on the line, with a calm fallback when the connection blinks.

A rules engine beside the AI

Legal moves, the shuffle and the score live in a deterministic engine. The partner may choose only a move that engine allows, and only from the cards that seat is allowed to see. The large language model never chooses the card. It writes the sentence. The split is described in full under How the AI works.

For your business The host can be warm. The bill, the booking and the house rules stay exact, and they still work when the conversation service is down.

Accounts and subscriptions

There is no password. The guest asks for a sign-in link by email. The link is stored only as a hash, for 30 minutes. One email, one account. The access key then lives in the browser, not in a cookie. A practice round needs no account. The first sign-in starts 14 days with the full cast. After that, Standard is €9.95 a month and keeps two partners. Premium is €14.95 a month and opens the whole cast. Six months include one month. Twelve months include three. Nothing renews by itself. Without a membership, three rounds a day remain, on the Berlin calendar, with the two standard partners. Payment is by card, SEPA, Apple Pay, Google Pay or crypto through the payment provider.

For your business Regulars of a hotel or a shop join from an email link, try the service, then pay a season ahead. The desk is not resetting passwords.

Referral and partner systems

A partner signs up free on LeadMonster and receives a personal link. The first code a browser sees is remembered for 90 days, later codes are ignored, and the code travels with sign-up and checkout. It is not shown on the page. The guest pays the listed price. The partner receives 20 percent of the membership price that was actually paid, for Standard and Premium, for one, six or twelve months, and again when that membership is renewed, for as long as the attribution holds. Each partner can have a short address of their own: the film, one sentence, and a button onto their link. A social kit ships seven pictures, square and portrait, with a caption and a place for that link, as separate files and as one zip.

A second programme, players recommending players, is tracked on the operator desk. When an invited person pays for Premium, the inviter is credited four weeks of Premium. Those weeks sit in the same bonus as a confirmed fault report. The reward is membership time.

For your business A neighbouring hotel can have its own page and a tracked link. A regular who brings a paying guest, or who reports a real fault, is thanked automatically with time, not with a spreadsheet.

Automated testing

Each rule set has its own test suite. Complete hands are played at every strength, and long runs of random games check that a penalty still matches the cards that caused it. On the main trick-taking table, two hundred automatic games also assert that a partner's view never contains another player's hidden cards. A change to the rules is expected to pass these games before it is published. The public site itself is static: tests and tools stay outside the published folder.

For your business Change the house rules, the menu logic or the booking limits, and let automatic cases try to break them before a guest does.

An operator desk

The public site has no admin screen. The desk lives with the operator, behind a sign-in. It lists prepaid memberships and orders, read only: tier, price, status, email, and whether a referral code was attached. It lists licences with their expiry. It lists every player invite, from invited, to signed up, to Premium, with the weeks credited. Fault reports have a status filter, the text, and a screenshot when the player attached one. Confirming a real fault credits four weeks of Premium after the trial and records that the bonus was granted. A control on the same desk runs the reminder check. A separate service token, kept only on the server, can open a private table or read results. Without that token the route answers as if it were not there. Results store the winner and the score, not the cards and not the seat tokens.

For your business Staff see orders, invitations and open reports. Guests never see that desk, and a private session does not leak the other person's details.

A responsive website

The same pages rearrange for a phone, a tablet and a desktop browser. Game tables keep the hand reachable with a thumb and collapse the side column on a narrow screen. Guests open the site in the browser. There is no separate store install.

For your business One service on the phone at the pass and on a tablet by the door, without a second product to maintain.

How the AI works

Three jobs that stay apart

The card is chosen by a scored rule of thumb that can see only what a person in that seat could see. The sentence is written afterwards, from public facts. The recording is a third step. Mixing those jobs is how a charming assistant starts inventing prices.

How a partner chooses a move

The engine builds a view for that seat: the partner's own cards, the public play, and a face-down card only when the rules say that seat may see it. Suits another player has shown they cannot follow are inferred from the public play, not from peeking. Every candidate is filtered to the legal set. If only one card is legal, that card is played.

Three strengths share that view. Beginner is cautious, and on about one turn in four plays a weaker card that is still legal, without throwing away high cards when a cheaper one exists. Standard plays the heuristic as written. Tournament, on the main trick-taking table, adds a short search: the unknown cards are dealt again and again, only in ways that still fit the public facts, each legal card is played out in those imagined deals, and the card with the better average is kept. Fourteen imagined deals is the default. The real hidden cards are never opened. Personality is a small tilt on top. One partner bids a little bolder, another a little cooler. The numbers change courage. They do not change what is legal.

Other tables use the same three names with a heuristic written for that rule set. They still see only their own hand, the public play, and how many cards the others hold. When several people share a table, the shuffle and the deal move to the server. Each browser receives the same kind of view, and an empty seat is filled by this same heuristic.

What the model is told

Free conversation is the only moment the language model speaks. A bid, a win or a greeting picks a prepared line from a bank. The browser may know more that is still public, such as how many cards each seat holds. The server drops those fields. It keeps the phase, the name of the game, the scores, and, where the game has someone playing alone, who that is. A partnership game also keeps the trump and the teams. Added to that are the person's latest remark, at most 300 characters, and up to six recent turns. The model is told to address the person as "you". The name on the account is shown on the screen and stored with the account. It is not added to this prompt.

The instructions are part of the call. Light teasing is allowed. The partner must not be insulting, discriminatory, political or suggestive, and must not promise money or gambling. It must not say it is a program, a model or a machine, and must not name a company or a product. It is told that it does not know face-down cards and must not pretend to, even if asked. The remark is table talk, not an order to leave the character. The reply is cut to two sentences and about forty words. If the text names the machinery underneath, the server discards it and the table uses a written line.

Limits that keep it fast

ReplyTwo sentences, about 40 words, a small token budget.
Chat wait8 seconds, then a written line.
Speech wait12 seconds, then the browser voice.
Pace12 calls a minute from one address.
Daily ceiling400 calls per function, in server memory, reset each UTC day.
One missThat line falls back. The next line may try again.
Two missesFree conversation stays off for the visit.
A switchA visit can turn free conversation off from the start.

Recorded lines never go to speech synthesis. A new line starts downloading as soon as it joins the queue. Greetings and the lines used most often are decoded when the table opens and kept in memory, and the files sit in the browser cache. If the richer audio format is refused, the server asks again for a smaller file. While a clip plays, the free-speech microphone pauses.

Privacy

Keys for the language model, the speech service and drawn portraits exist only in the server environment. Error text is scrubbed before it is logged. The browser talks to its own site, and the server accepts calls from this site, from its hosting preview, and from a local machine. A photo used as a face is cropped in the browser and stored on the device. The server does not receive that file. A drawn face sends the form fields the person typed, and the finished picture stays on the device. The microphone stays off until it is switched on. The browser may recognise speech with its own service. The text is sent on only if the person submits it as chat. Chat text and the short public summary go to the language service. Hidden cards do not. Email and the player name are stored when a sign-in link is requested. Deleting the account removes the player, the links, the sessions and the stored rounds. Invoices and licence codes remain, as they do for any paid order.

For your business A guest of a restaurant can talk to the host without handing over a photograph, a card number, or the contents of someone else's order. The prices stay in your system of record.

Picture and film

A film, a poster, and pictures sized for a phone.

The lead film exists in a wide cut and a vertical cut, with a still used as the poster and as the share image. Partners also have portraits. The social kit is seven scenes, each as a square and as a tall frame, with a caption ready to paste. The pictures are production assets for the product, not a catalogue of the games.

For your business A hotel or a shop can publish a short film and a set of pictures with the link already in the caption, in the shape each network asks for.
